New to pellet stoves?

Common Questions From New Owners

If you recently bought a pellet stove, purchased a home with one already installed, or are using one for the first time, these answers explain why regular service, maintenance, and timely repairs matter.

How often should a pellet stove be cleaned or serviced?

A pellet stove should be cleaned regularly during the heating season, with a more thorough cleaning and inspection performed periodically. Ash, soot, and pellet dust can build up in areas that are not obvious during normal use. Keeping the stove clean helps maintain proper airflow, dependable operation, and efficient heat output.

Why does my pellet stove need professional service if I clean the ash myself?

Emptying the burn pot and ash pan is important, but there are internal areas and components that also need attention. Blowers, exhaust passages, sensors, motors, seals, and venting can collect debris or develop problems over time. A more complete service can identify issues that routine homeowner cleaning may not reveal.

What are the signs that my pellet stove may need repair?

Common warning signs include trouble starting, pellets not feeding properly, weak or unusual flames, excessive smoke or soot, strange noises, poor heat output, the stove shutting itself down, or error codes. If the stove suddenly starts behaving differently than normal, it is worth having the problem diagnosed before it becomes a larger repair.

I just bought a home with a pellet stove. Should I have it checked before using it?

Yes, especially if you do not know its maintenance history. A previous owner may not have cleaned or serviced it regularly, and you may not know the condition of the venting, seals, motors, or other components. An inspection can give a new owner a better understanding of the stove's condition before relying on it for heat.

I'm buying a used pellet stove. What should I know before installing or using it?

Used pellet stoves can be a good option, but age and condition matter. Components such as auger motors, igniters, blowers, switches, control boards, door seals, and exhaust systems can wear over time. Knowing the stove's make and model and having its condition evaluated can help identify problems before you depend on it during cold weather.

Why isn't my pellet stove producing as much heat as it used to?

Reduced heat can come from several things, including restricted airflow, dirty internal passages, worn seals, blower problems, pellet-feed issues, poor-quality fuel, or components that are not operating correctly. Cleaning and troubleshooting the stove can help determine whether the problem is maintenance-related or something that needs repair.

Is preventative maintenance worth it if the stove is still working?

Usually, yes. A stove can continue running while airflow becomes restricted or components begin wearing down. Routine maintenance gives you a chance to address smaller issues before they turn into a breakdown, especially before the coldest part of the heating season.
